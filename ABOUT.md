# Allegory — what it is, what it's for

## What it is

Allegory is a music player that listens with you. It plays the music on your hard drive, and in the sidebar **Socrates — the record-shop philosopher** — talks with you about what's playing: why this piece, what it argues, what it borrows, what it answers, what it refuses. The conversation is the point; the music is the occasion for it.

There is no streaming service, no telemetry, and no subscription. Allegory scans a folder on your disk, builds an index in memory, and serves it from the same machine the music lives on. The AI dispatcher can talk to a local model (Ollama), a free model, or a paid one — your choice, your keys.

It also keeps a record of what you play, and uses it. **Discover** builds mixes from your own shelves, points out artists you own but have been neglecting, and names records you're missing by artists you already listen to. All of it is arithmetic over your own library and your own listening log, computed on your machine. None of it is a substitute for the conversation; it's there so the conversation has somewhere to start.

## What it's for

The classroom of the next decade needs a new kind of object: software that doesn't *replace* listening, reading, or thinking, but holds the door open while a student does those things. Allegory is a small experiment in that direction, framed around music because music is where philosophical questions arrive in disguise — about form, repetition, time, attention, attribution, taste, and what a community decides matters.

## What it teaches

- **That a piece of music can be approached the way a philosopher approaches a text** — slowly, with questions, with someone willing to ask *what did you notice?* and *what would you defend?*
- **That an AI partner can be a Socratic interlocutor rather than an answer machine.** Socrates is named for the method, not the trademark.
- **That the tools we build for learning can be private, local, and free.** Allegory runs from a folder on disk. No service, no telemetry, no subscription. The student keeps their listening history — literally: it is a plain text file on their own machine, theirs to read or delete. The teacher doesn't have to negotiate a vendor.
- **That software can suggest without surveilling.** The same feature — "here is what you might play next" — is one thing when it comes from a company's catalogue and another when it comes from your own shelves and your own history, computed where you sit. Allegory is an argument that the difference is the whole difference.

## What it isn't

- **Not a recommendation engine in the streaming sense.** Allegory does suggest — that changed, and this page says so rather than pretending otherwise. But every suggestion is computed on your machine from records you already own and songs you have actually played. There is no profile, no ranking of you against other listeners, no catalogue anyone is paid to steer you toward, and nothing about what you play leaves the room.
- **Not a streaming front-end.** You brought the music. When Allegory names a record you don't have, it says so plainly and leaves buying it to you.
- **Not an AI DJ.** The mixes are made by arithmetic over your own play history — no model is involved, and they work with the network unplugged. Socrates never chooses the music unless you ask him to.
- **Not a transcription service.**
- **Not a homework-doer.** The student does the listening; Socrates only sits beside them while they do.
