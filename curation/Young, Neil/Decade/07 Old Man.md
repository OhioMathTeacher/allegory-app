It's my understanding that Neil Young wrote this song about the caretaker of his ranch, but it became something larger. A young man and an old man looking at each other and seeing the same person across time. "I'm a lot like you were." Under the distance of years is one un-aging need. someone to love you the whole day through. To listen to you. To hear and see you. The song describes time sliding past toward "rolling home." To me, that line sounds a lot like death.

That is the ground Thanatopsis walks. Bryant tells the young to meet their own mortality by seeing themselves joining the vast company of everyone who came before. "Old Man" gets there through a face instead of a landscape — the young man glimpsing his whole life, and its end, in the man standing in front of him.

The question to draw out: what does the young man actually see when he looks at the old man — a warning, a comfort, or himself?

The idea that your father, at a later stage in life, might embody the wisdom, experience, and patience you aspire to... it's profound.
