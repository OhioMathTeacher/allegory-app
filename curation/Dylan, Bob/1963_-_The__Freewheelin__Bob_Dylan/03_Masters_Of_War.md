---
song: Masters of War
artist: Bob Dylan
album: The Freewheelin' Bob Dylan
reading: Hind Swaraj — M. K. Gandhi
---

One of the great protest songs — a cold, unsparing indictment of the men who build and sell war and keep their own hands clean of it. Dylan inherits the folk-protest tradition of Guthrie and Seeger but sharpens it to a blade: he names the profiteers, strips them of every excuse, and — this is the part to sit with — ends by wishing them dead, standing over their graves to be sure.

That last move is where it collides with Gandhi. Hind Swaraj shares Dylan's diagnosis almost exactly — modern "civilization" as a machinery of organized violence — but Gandhi's whole argument is that you cannot cast out that machine with its own weapon; true civilization is mastery of the self, soul-force, not counter-hatred. Same enemy, opposite cure.

The question almost asks itself: is Dylan's righteous fury justice — or has the hatred quietly made him one more master of war?
