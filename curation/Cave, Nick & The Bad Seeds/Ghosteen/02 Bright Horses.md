---
song: Bright Horses
artist: Nick Cave & The Bad Seeds
album: Ghosteen
reading: The Allegory of the Cave — Plato (the counterpoint to "I Found Out")
---

A song about longing, loss, and a strange kind of freedom — the bright horses breaking loose from the fields. Cave has described an inner negotiation between his cynical and his empathetic selves: the middle of the song sees the world coldly, the end chooses to believe in it anyway.

This is the believer's answer to the disenchanted. Where Lennon's "I Found Out" tears every consoling illusion down, Cave keeps his — knowing they may be illusions. Writing in the Red Hand Files about his late son, he said grief "trails bright phantoms in its wake": a longing strong enough to build meaning where meaning may not exist, to see the missing one in horses, trains, light on a wall.

For the cave: the freed prisoner has seen the shadows for what they are — and walks back in, on purpose, because the shadow is his son. Worth asking the listener: is that weakness, or the bravest thing a person can do?
